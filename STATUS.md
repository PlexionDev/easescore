# EaseScore.AI — status

_Plain-language build log. Newest first._

## Now
- Loading Allegheny County data into the database (assessments → parcel boundaries → zoning → hazards → sales).

## Done
- Land price now uses the mapped lot (the GIS outline the pane, report and site plan show); the assessor's recorded lot is used only when there is no outline. Some assessor records list only part of the lot (one showed 1,200 sq ft on a 6,764 sq ft lot), which had priced the land far too low. Report Section 6 (existing-home and land sales) now shows block-level addresses on screen, like the rest of the app; full street addresses only in the PDF.
- Comparable-sales grid and confidence grades. The Feasibility Study (Section 9) shows up to 10 recent new-construction sales from the same set the sale value comes from (same home type first, then nearest, then most recent): distance, date, price, type, finished sq ft, $/sq ft and year built, with the selection rule, the search steps and one template reconciliation line; the median and middle half are the pro forma's own numbers. On screen and in the Pencil calculator, addresses show only the block ("1200 block of ..."); full street addresses appear only in the PDF. With fewer than 5 sales it says so, shows how far the search widened, and never uses older homes as the value. Section 1 has a confidence table (Zoning, Site geometry, Geotechnical, Construction cost, Sale revenue, Financing, Schedule, Overall: Preliminary) graded by fixed rules that are printed under it. Beds, baths and parking are not in the County sale records, so the grid says they are not shown.
- Decision box at the top of the Pencil calculator and the Feasibility Study (same engine function, same numbers in the PDF). Editable investment criteria (target profit 15% of cost, contingency at least 10%, loan at most 75% of cost; kept in the link as dc_*). It shows gross sellout, selling and closing (seller's half of the transfer tax; commissions stay excluded), net proceeds, total cost, developer profit on cost and on revenue, profit before loan interest (unlevered), the sale price per home and per sq ft needed for the target and for zero profit, the cost cut needed, and the residual land value (the most you could pay for the land and still hit the target) next to the land price used and its source; a negative residual is shown as negative. One template sentence states the result. The report also shows property tax today vs. after completion (assessment ratio from completed projects × 2026 millage; abatements not modeled) and a unit-by-unit sellout table (size, bedrooms by size band, parking, price range per home, a labeled closing schedule and the loan interest until the last closing). Rentals now read "unlevered" instead of "depends on the loan".
- New cost model (cost assumptions v0.2), tested against real homes first. Construction is now the cost to build, with the builder's fee removed: Standard infill $190 per sq ft by default (Basic $160, Mid-range $240, High-end $320, Custom $420), each shown next to the builders' published retail range it comes from. The hillside premium and the structural engineer fire only when the slope under the building (lidar under the QuickFit footprint) is over 15%; 25% or more adds the steep rate and retaining walls. New lines: water and sewer laterals $10,000 per house, survey, civil/grading when a big area is disturbed, geotechnical report ($7,000 landslide-prone or steep, $4,000 undermined), builder's risk insurance, title and closing. City permits follow the PLI 2026 fee schedule; outside the City a flat $12,000 per house is flagged "confirm with the municipality." Loan 7.75%, 55% drawn, 10 months; no sales commissions; City taxes at the 2026 total of 27.307 mills. The budget, report and /methods carry the new cost disclaimer, and /methods states the backtest on 515 real homes (median 53% above sale prices under the old model; roughly break-even on 96 single-lot infill homes now; not calibrated for subdivision homes). Stored parcel results were regenerated for the 96 demo and public parcels; Ease Scores did not change.
- Parcel pane fits one screen (1440×900) with no scrolling: the address (or "No official address · near <street>") with the parcel ID, a photo slot beside a small site layout (lot, setbacks, buildable area and the best option's footprint from QuickFit), the Ease Score with its band and one sentence on why, four buildability tiles (lot size, average slope, hazards, zoning), the best option in one line, at most two review callouts, and three buttons: Open QuickFit, Pencil calculator, Full report. Factor bars and receipts moved to "Score details", Best options and Street precedent to a panel opened from QuickFit (and into the report's Zoning section), the checklist to its own panel and the report. The page and QuickFit open on the top-ranked option. The photo slot shows Google Street View when a panorama is within 60 m of the lot; Street View Static is not enabled on our Google key yet, so today it shows our own 3D map of the lot, labeled "Illustrative 3D map, not a photo".
- QuickFit shows its layout within about 2 seconds even when its background worker is slow to load (it solves the first layout on the page itself), and no longer restarts the worker when the map data arrives. The Pencil calculator opens as its own panel with build quality first (Standard infill $190 per sq ft by default, cost to build), then the result, the QuickFit layout it prices and the editable budget lines. The numbers bar (QuickFit only) reads ranges low to high with a real minus sign, e.g. "Gap −$510K to −$100K".
- Parcel page views are now four separate tabs: 3D (photoreal, the default: the real place in Google 3D with the lot outlined and a slow orbit that stops when you touch it), QuickFit 3D (the clay model, plan, controls and the numbers bar, only here), Terrain (lidar ground and contours) and 2D (flat analysis map with slope classes, zoning and hazards). Only the open tab runs, which keeps the page from stalling. Point at a neighboring lot in 3D, Terrain or 2D to see its address (or "No official address"), parcel ID, zoning and owner type; click it to open that parcel. Keyboard users get the same through "Nearby parcels" in Describe this view.
- Accessibility pass. Every map and 3D view on the parcel page has a "Describe this view" panel that says in words what the picture shows (lot size, slope, hazard shares, setbacks, the studied building), built from the same numbers. The Planner, Policy and Nonprofit maps have a Table view. Maps and the 3D view work from the keyboard (arrow keys move, + and − zoom), sliders have − and + buttons so nothing needs dragging, screen readers hear new results after a filter, lever or what-if change, and reduced-motion settings turn off the fly-in, orbit and animations. The Feasibility Study PDF is tagged, with bookmarks, its language set and alt text on the site plan and every chart. An automated WCAG 2.2 AA scan finds no violations on the main pages (home, parcel page, report, the three seats and the docs pages). New page: /accessibility, an honest statement of what works and what doesn't yet.
- QuickFit v2 replaces the first QuickFit on the parcel page. "Build it in 3D" is now the first view: a clay model of the building on the real lidar ground with its driveway, garage, windows and the neighboring houses, or a plan drawing where you can click a lot edge to make it the front. Controls: building type (each shows Allowed / Needs approval / Not allowed / Doesn't fit), stories, width, depth, parking and "what if the setbacks were…" boxes (blank = code). The sentence under it names the rule that limits the building, and approvals list past Zoning Board outcomes. The same layout feeds the option list and score, "Does it pencil?", the metrics bar, the report and its site plan. Google photoreal 3D is now the "Context" view. Checks: the solver's own tests plus 15 real public lots run with every engine test.
- QuickFit is now an instant 3D generator on the parcel page. A "Build it in 3D" panel floats over the map: building type (single-family, duplex, 3–4 units, townhouse row, ADU), stories, unit width, parking (pad, tuck-under, none) and setback what-ifs. Every change re-solves in the browser (a Web Worker, about 7–10 ms per solve once warm) and redraws the building as colored floor-and-unit blocks with stair cores, garage bays and a parking pad, in the photoreal view and in 3D Terrain, with the code setback line on the ground. On slopes steeper than 15% under the building, the floors step down the lidar grade in 2.5 ft steps, and the pro forma prices a stepped foundation once (it never adds to the steep-slope cost the lot already carries). A bar along the bottom shows homes, average home size, gross area, parking, total cost, value or yearly rent, profit or gap, yield on cost and the rule that limits the layout, all from the same pro forma as "Does it pencil?"; up to three schemes can be pinned and compared side by side. Changing the building type switches the option above the factor bars, and the score, summary and pro forma follow. The old plan diagram in "Change the plan" is replaced by a small thumbnail and a summary. Screens: .planning/screens/quickfit3d-*.png (private).
- Credibility pass on the parcel page: the Ease Score is now v0.2. A lot that is at least half landslide-prone or 40% steeper than 25% scores no better than Moderate and says why; any layout that needs a variance tops out at 90 on zoning; variance odds use the district's record (5+ cases), else the citywide record with its case count and years, else a labeled default. One layout per option ("SelectedScheme") now feeds the score, the pro forma, the summary, QuickFit's "Priced layout" card and the 3D massing, and the page opens on the summary's featured by-right option. The pro forma shows every line and total as a range (low–high, likely), with a source badge per line (amber = our assumption, edit it), a small strip comparing sources (Pittsburgh builders, NAHB national, local projects), and the year of the land, value and rent data.
- Parcel page speed: the pane now reads one precomputed row per parcel (address, parcel ID with a copy button, then neighborhood and zoning at the top) and shows in under half a second on a production build, where it used to wait 4 to 16 seconds for every database call, the score and a county-wide sales download. The map and 3D view load alongside without holding up the pane. Parcels without a stored row are still computed live. Server step times are logged, and /api/pane/<parcel ID> reports them as a Server-Timing header.
- The photoreal 3D view works in production builds again (a Cesium module broke the minified bundle; dev mode was fine).
- Feasibility Study PDF: Figure "site plan" is now sheet EA-101, a true-scale engineering-style drawing on its own landscape page (standard scale 1" = 10' to 40', enlarged and noted when a lot is too big): lot lines with dimensions, zoning setbacks, buildable area, the studied footprint with party walls and an estimated floor elevation, neighboring buildings, curb/walk/right-of-way at typical widths (labeled as an assumption), 2-foot lidar contours, 25%+ slope hatch, hazard limits as lines, numbered keynotes, legend, north arrow and title block. When nothing fits, the sheet still draws and a keynote says why. The whole report was also reworked for printing: white pages, outlined callouts, gray charts with one accent color, fewer half-empty pages.
- "Does it pencil?" is live on the parcel page and in the Feasibility Study PDF. Cost defaults live in one versioned file (now cost assumptions v0.2, see above): five construction tiers, hillside adders that fire from the lidar slope with a plain reason, the mine grouting or insurance path, soft costs, contingency (flat, hillside or rehab) and loan terms. Every value shows its source label and can be edited on the page. Items that apply but have no local cost yet (demolition, dumpsters) are listed as "Not included" and the estimate is marked partial, never counted as zero. The page shows the budget, the sale value or rent, profit or yield on cost, the math as sentences, and a check against recent Allegheny County projects; the PDF fills sections 7 to 11 and Appendix C from the same code.
- Parcel page shows the Ease Score v0.1: score and band per housing option (best by default, switchable), red flags banner, the four plain-English answers, amber "Review required" items with citations and costs, factor bars with data receipts, months to a permit, the planning badge, and "What would unlock it".
- Water service: every parcel tagged with its public water system from PA DEP service-area maps (577,131 inside a service area, 4,866 clearly outside, 3,354 near a boundary). Sewer service marked unknown: no public sewer service-area map exists for the county.

## Differences between the handout and the code

1. **Full report vs. evidence.** The code sets one standard for LS-O: evidence based on a field investigation by a registered professional. The two tiers ("full report" for new construction and multi-story additions, "evidence" for small work) are DCP practice, not code text.
2. **"Memo" is not explicit.** The word appears only in a section heading.
3. **Subsidence history (UM-O).** The code requires both more than 100 ft of overburden **and** no known subsidence history nearby before a single-unit dwelling can skip the investigation. The handout mentions only overburden.
4. **Scope of the single-family exemption (UM-O).** The code extends it to structures comparable to a single-unit dwelling. The handout says "single family dwellings".
5. **Steep slopes.** The code (§915.02.A.1.c) also requires terraces or walls at intervals of not less than 10 ft, approved in the report.
6. **Citation quirks.** The handout cites "§906.5" (meaning §906.05) and "§915.02.A.1" (the text is at .A.1.c). The code itself (§906.04.B.3) cross-references "Section 906.03.B.2" for the LS-O site investigation, which looks like a stale internal reference. The substantive requirement is at §906.04.B.2.
7. **Professionals.** The code says "registered professional" or "geotechnical consultant". The handout lists four professional types, including geologists. Neither requires a PE exclusively.

## Implications for the requirements engine

- **LS-O rule, two tiers.** New construction or a multi-story addition produces a "full geotechnical report (with borings)". A parking pad, deck, porch, stoop, balcony, retaining wall or one-story addition produces a "geotechnical professional's evidence (letter or memo) that the work will not increase landslide susceptibility". Label the second tier "letter/memo (per DCP practice)", not as a code requirement. Cite the handout for the tiers and §906.04.B.2 for the underlying rule.
- **LS-O exemptions.** Fences of 6 ft or less, interior-only work, like-for-like deck renovation, sheds under 120 sq ft, and exterior repair with no envelope change should be "likely not required". Flip them to required if the work changes the slope or sits near the slope edge. Treat this as "may not be required", never as a guaranteed exemption.
- **UM-O rule.** New construction or enlargement always requires a Coal Status Report. Then:
  - Single-family or comparable structure, with overburden over 100 ft and no known nearby subsidence: no geotechnical report.
  - Single-family with overburden of 100 ft or less, or with any known subsidence history nearby: geotechnical report required.
  - Anything larger or heavier than a single-family dwelling, or a public-hazard use: geotechnical report always required.
  - Include the subsidence-history check from the code, even though the handout omits it. The county mine subsidence data already loaded can help flag it.
- **Steep slope rule.** It applies citywide, not only in overlays. The trigger is **proposed finished cut or fill slopes over 25%**, not the existing grade. An existing-grade test (for example lidar slope ≥ 25%) should produce a "likely" flag, not a determination. If the rule fires, also flag the terracing condition (walls or terraces at intervals of not less than 10 ft; retaining walls of 10 ft or less per §915.02.A.1.e).
- **Preparer.** Do not say "PE required". Say "credentialed, insured geotechnical professional (soil, civil or geotechnical engineer, or professional/engineering geologist); signed and sealed with insurance information".
- **Timing.** The report is submitted at zoning/site plan review (DCP completeness check) and reviewed again by PLI with the building permit. Model the cost and schedule as a pre-zoning-approval item. Borings mean site access and heavy equipment.
- **Reuse.** An existing approved report may be reused when the soil impact does not increase. Make this an optional input.
- **Provenance.** Store the handout URL with its "December 2024" date, and the ecode360 version date ("legislation through 09-16-2026"), on each rule. The handout disclaims that it is not law. Code text controls where the two differ.

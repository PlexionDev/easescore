# When Is a Geotechnical Report Required? Pittsburgh and Allegheny County

This note covers one question: when does the law actually **require** a geotechnical, soils or subsurface report for a house or a small multifamily project in the City of Pittsburgh and in the rest of Allegheny County, Pennsylvania?

Each rule below has a short paraphrase, the section number and a source URL. Nothing here is legal advice. Where the text leaves something to an official's judgment, the note says so and does not guess at a threshold.

**Currency.** Sources were read on **2026-09-26**.
- City of Pittsburgh Code of Ordinances (ecode360): "Includes legislation through 09-16-2026."
- Allegheny County Code (ecode360, Chapter 780 County SALDO): "Includes legislation through 06-04-2024."
- Mt. Lebanon Code: legislation through 02-10-2026.
- Monroeville Code: legislation through 06-09-2026.
- Penn Hills Code: legislation through 07-20-2026.
- PA Uniform Construction Code: 34 Pa. Code §403.21 as amended Nov. 7, 2025, **effective Jan. 1, 2026**. It adopts the **2021** IBC and IRC. PLI policy EP-2025-01 (revised 12/15/25) says PLI applies the 2021 ICC base codes to applications it accepts on or after Jan. 1, 2026.
- Municode no longer carries the Pittsburgh code, so all City sections come from ecode360.

## 1. Three kinds of "requirement"

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **REQUIRED** | The code text says a report, investigation or engineer-sealed analysis *shall* be submitted when a stated condition is met. No official has to ask for it. |
| **DISCRETIONARY** | The code gives an official (building official, Zoning Administrator, Code Official, municipal engineer or planning body) the power to require it. The text sets no fixed numeric trigger, or lists factors only. |
| **RECOMMENDED** | Guidance, advisory wording ("strongly recommended"), agency publications or industry practice. There is no legal duty. |

**Legal authority.** The PA Municipalities Planning Code lets municipalities set these requirements:
- **§503(2)(v)** (53 P.S. §10503): SALDOs must provide that land subject to flooding, subsidence or underground fires is made safe or set aside.
- **§503(1)**: plat submission specifications.
- **§605(2)(iii)**: zoning classifications for "places of relatively steep slope or grade, or other areas of hazardous geological or topographic features."

The MPC text does not use the words "geotechnical" or "landslide." Municipal geotechnical requirements rest on these general grants.

## 3. Undermined areas: statutory mine-subsidence rules

- **The Bituminous Mine Subsidence and Land Conservation Act (BMSLCA)**, Act of 1966 (1st Sp. Sess.) P.L.31, No.1, as amended, regulates coal *operators*. §14 (52 P.S. §1406.14) requires a deed notice about surface support rights. No BMSLCA provision was found that requires a surface builder to investigate before building.
- **PA DEP Mine Subsidence Insurance** is voluntary: "the choice of coverage ... is always left to the applicant." Buildings in areas with a record of past problems need an **MSI inspection before coverage can be bought**. This is an insurance eligibility rule, not a permit rule.
- **Bottom line.** A subsidence investigation is legally **required** only where a local ordinance says so: Pittsburgh §906.05, County SALDO §780-510.B, Mt. Lebanon §6-302/303 (a mine-records summary), and Monroeville §243-10. Otherwise it is **recommended** or discretionary.

## 4. "Red beds" (Conemaugh Group)

- **Pomeroy (USGS Professional Paper 1229, 1982)** names red mudstone and claystone near the top of the **Glenshaw Formation ("Pittsburgh red beds")**, and in parts of the **Casselman Formation ("Clarksburg red beds")**, as key to slumping and earthflow. Its stability table rates the Pittsburgh red beds moderately high to severe.
- **DCNR Educational Series 9** (Delano and Wilshusen, 2001) calls greater Pittsburgh one of the most severely affected landslide regions in the U.S. It links this to weak claystone bedrock. It says site stability should be judged by a qualified person and that a few local governments have adopted landslide rules. It does not name "red beds," and it states no legal requirement.
- **Regulatory consequence: none that names red beds.** No City, County or state code section found names the red beds, Casselman or Glenshaw. The rules key instead on:
  - Pittsburgh's mapped LS-O district (§906.04);
  - Title 13's definition of "landslide-prone area" (the LS-O map "and/or" a geotechnical study; §1302.01);
  - the County's soil survey and Landslide Susceptibility Map (§780-510.A);
  - municipal "landslide-prone soils" language (Monroeville, Mt. Lebanon, Penn Hills).

## 5. Summary

**City of Pittsburgh: legally required, with no official's request needed**
1. The site is in the **LS-O** (landslide-prone) overlay and the work needs any excavation, fill or vegetation removal. A field investigation by a registered professional or geotechnical consultant is required (§906.04).
2. The site is in the **UM-O** (undermined) overlay and has **100 ft of overburden or less**, known nearby subsidence, or a building larger or heavier than a typical house (this can include small multifamily). A site investigation is required (§906.05). Every UM-O project must also submit the available DEP mine data.
3. The design has a **cut or fill slope steeper than 25%** anywhere in the City. A geotechnical investigation report is required (§915.02.A.1.c).
4. The design has cuts steeper than 1.5:1 or fills steeper than 2:1. A registered professional's written recommendation is required (§1003.17).
5. The project needs a **SWM Site Plan** (10,000 sf disturbed or 5,000 sf new impervious). An infiltration and geotechnical testing report is required for stormwater design (§1304.03).
6. A **landslide has occurred** on the property. Remediation must be based on a PE-sealed geotechnical report (IPMC §302.2.1 as amended in §1004.02).
7. For **IBC buildings** (typically multifamily with 3 or more units that are not townhouses), IBC §1803.2 treats an investigation as required unless the official waives it. It is also required for fill deeper than 12 in., deep foundations, a basement near groundwater (unless waterproofed), or excavation that undermines an adjacent foundation.

**City of Pittsburgh: only if an official asks.** H-District soils report (§911.04.A.69); IRC §R401.4 soil test; IBC §1803.5.2; Chapter 1003 soils and stability data; PLI's "may be required" for steep, landslide-prone or undermined sites; slope-setback alternates.

**Suburbs.** There is no countywide geotechnical rule for a single house. The 26 municipalities under the **County SALDO** require a PE geotechnical analysis for mapped or soil-survey unstable land, graded slopes over 2:1 (one of three paths), and undermined land with 100 ft of overburden or less. Other municipalities set their own triggers, and these vary widely:
- **Mt. Lebanon**: 20% slope (a report is required on 20–40% and over-40% sites).
- **Monroeville**: 3H:1V or 30% slope, landslide-prone or subsidence-prone land, or more than 10,000 cu yd.
- **Penn Hills**: discretionary Environmental Impact Statement at 25%; subsurface investigation required under permitted fills.

State E&S rules (5,000 sf plan, 1 acre NPDES) do not require geotechnical reports anywhere.

## 6. Could not confirm

- **The legal status of the County "Landslide Susceptibility Map of Allegheny County"** (§780-510.A): its edition and where it is published.
